# Regulatory Review Notes

Last reviewed: September 1, 2026

These notes document material differences between the original training source text and current official guidance used in the interactive Module 1.1 draft. They are intended to make future content review easier and to keep changes transparent.

## 42 CFR Part 2

The original source text reflects an older, broadly restrictive summary of Part 2 redisclosure. HHS's 2024 Part 2 Final Rule became mandatory for compliance on February 16, 2026 and aligned important parts of Part 2 with HIPAA, including a single consent for future treatment, payment, and health care operations uses/disclosures in applicable circumstances.

Part 2 still provides strong protections, including restrictions on use of SUD records in legal proceedings against a patient and separate requirements for SUD counseling notes.

Official source:
- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/part-2/index.html
- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/fact-sheet-42-cfr-part-2-final-rule/index.html

## HIPAA breach response

The source text stated that the clinic must investigate within 30 days. HIPAA does not impose a universal 30-day breach-investigation deadline.

An impermissible use or disclosure is generally presumed to be a breach unless an exception applies or the covered entity/business associate demonstrates a low probability that PHI was compromised using the four-factor risk assessment in 45 CFR 164.402.

When individual notification is required, HIPAA requires notification without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 calendar days after discovery.

Official source:
- https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/breach-notification/index.html

Clinic policy may and should impose a much faster internal employee reporting requirement.

## Washington My Health My Data Act

The source text described MHMD as applying "in addition to HIPAA." That shorthand can be misleading. Washington's Attorney General describes the Act as the first privacy-focused law in the country designed to protect personal health data that falls outside HIPAA's ambit. The statute also contains exemptions and entity/data-specific rules.

Interactive training therefore teaches staff to follow the clinic's approved MHMD/privacy workflow rather than assuming every item of HIPAA PHI is governed identically by MHMD.

Official source:
- https://www.atg.wa.gov/protecting-washingtonians-personal-health-data-and-privacy

## Washington minor consent — STI care

The original source material listed STI testing/treatment under age 13+. RCW 70.24.110 provides that a minor age **14 or older** may consent to care related to diagnosis or treatment of an STI and treatment to avoid HIV infection.

Washington DOH's 2026 adolescent confidentiality materials use the same age-14 threshold.

Official sources:
- https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=70.24.110
- https://doh.wa.gov/you-and-your-family/adolescents-young-adults/confidentiality-roadmap-providers-serving-youth

## Washington minor consent — behavioral health

Washington DOH identifies outpatient mental health and outpatient substance-use-disorder treatment among services adolescents may consent to starting at age 13. RCW 71.34.530 states that an adolescent may request and receive outpatient treatment without parental consent, while parental authorization is required for a minor under age 13.

Official source:
- https://app.leg.wa.gov/rcw/default.aspx?cite=71.34.530

## Review cadence

Before deploying this content as formal required training, Inclusive Health should:

1. Confirm clinic-specific privacy policies and assigned Privacy/Security Officer roles.
2. Confirm EHR/portal workflows for adolescent confidential services.
3. Confirm how Part 2 applies to the clinic's actual programs, received records, and consent workflows.
4. Review the course annually and whenever a material federal or Washington rule changes.
5. Maintain course version, review date, and approval documentation.

This repository is training content and workflow tooling; it is not a substitute for legal advice or a clinic-specific compliance review.
